Gavin's Traffic Timeline

How did we get here? The truth is that Gavin once hated traffic as much as anyone. He was strongly in favor of telework when California government switched to remote during the COVID-19 pandemic, and remained a champion of telework for years, going as far as to encourage more state workers to start teleworking, and for state departments and agencies to cut back on office space to save taxpayer dollars. But that was before it all went horribly wrong. Here's what happened.

2020

May

During the May budget revision, Gavin talks about a plan to expand telework for state workers to save the state money.
“This transformation will allow for expanded long-term telework strategies, increased modernization and delivery of government services online, reconfigured office space, reduced leased space, and when possible, flexible work schedules for employees,” Newsom said.
Source: Will Keys for GovReport, June 5 2020, New Policy Aims for 75 Percent of State Workers to Telework, Report Says.

June

The Newsom announced a goal for 75% of state workers to telework. https://www.govreport.net/new-policy-aims-for-75-percent-of-state-workers-to-telework-report-says/

2021

April

Gavin announced he would make telework permanent for state workers and directed state agencies to use telework to achieve cost savings wherever possible. https://insider.govtech.com/california/news/newsom-says-state-workers-may-remain-remote.html

October

The California Department of General Services (DGS) published an update to Statewide Telework Policy https://oduinn.com/2021/10/13/californias-new-telework-policy-a-big-step-for-the-future-of-work/ https://web.archive.org/web/20211012235614/https://www.govreport.org/news/state-updates-telework-policy/

2022

January

DGS announces it will allow office leases to expire in favor of long-term remote work. https://www.governing.com/work/california-lets-23-leases-expire-as-workers-stay-remote

2023

July - December 2023, state departments report positive experiences and cost savings to DGS in an evaluation of telework. https://www.sacbee.com/news/politics-government/the-state-worker/article286611075.html In late 2023, state workers started to hear rumors that some departments would shift away from telework and direct workers to report to the office two days per week.

2024

January

Several departments issue official announcements to staff directing them to return to office. https://www.sacbee.com/news/politics-government/the-state-worker/article284319998.html Newsom proposes cutting state employee telework stiped to address budget shortfalls. https://www.sacbee.com/news/politics-government/the-state-worker/article284086788.html

February

Newsom's administration denies issuing a return to office order across state agencies after a department director indicates otherwise in an email about the policy change.
“This is a statewide policy impacting all agencies/departments under the Newsom Administration,” read a Feb. 12 email from Dr. Tomás Aragón, director of the California Department of Public Health, to all departmental staff.

Newsom’s office has repeatedly denied the existence of any formal “mandate” or “directive” that employees return to their offices two days per week. Instead, Gov. Gavin Newsom’s office insists that departments and agencies were instructed to “regularly evaluate” and “update” their telework policies “based on their individual needs.”

“Our office believes there is significant value from in-person work that should be considered along with the benefits of telework,” said Erin Mellon, the governor’s communications director, in a statement. Mellon declined to comment on why the CDPH memo included language that sounded as though the Newsom administration had issued a statewide in-office mandate.
Source: https://www.sacbee.com/news/politics-government/the-state-worker/article285705056.html#storylink=cpy
